## Finding 1 — The convergence claim rests on one item (BLOCKER)

The Weak Signal drafts assert:

> 8 items converging across 9 independent sources

The `Why it matters` paragraph — enterprise agent market unsettled, orchestration investment
running ahead of deployment, buyers hedging against lock-in — is derived **entirely from the
VentureBeat survey**, because `generator.py` takes `cluster[0].why_it_matters` (the lead item's
interpretation) and presents it as the interpretation of the whole cluster.

Item-by-item against that stated thesis:

| # | Item | Supports the thesis? |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | VentureBeat Pulse: enterprise agent orchestration survey | **Yes** |
| 2 | Output Reset — PPO/GRPO clipped-surrogate replacement (arXiv) | No — RL post-training math |
| 3 | Bloomy (YC S26) — K-12 mastery-learning tutor (HN) | No |
| 4 | `MisterBooo/llm-interview-questions` — 100 interview Qs, Chinese | No |
| 5 | VEHBench — vibration energy harvester design benchmark (arXiv) | No |
| 6 | The Register — spam filters evaded by text salting | No |
| 7 | 3D-Fit — 3D binding-molecule generation benchmark (arXiv) | No |
| 8 | `andy-library/customer-service-ai` — Spring AI orchestration repo | Tangential |

**One of eight.** A reader takes "8 items converging across 9 independent sources" to mean nine
outlets independently pointing at one phenomenon. What actually happened is that nine outlets each
mentioned LLMs.

### Root cause (precise)

`backend/app/digests/sections.py:206-208`:

```python
quadrant = [it for it in items if it.priority_class in HORIZON_CLASSES]
converging = [it for it in quadrant if it.convergence > 0]
weak_signals = _rank_weak(converging or quadrant, section_limit)
```

`it.convergence > 0` is a **per-item** property: this item's driving entity appears across
`>= GRAPH_MIN_SOURCES` independent sources. The filter collects items that each converge
**on their own separate entity** and presents them as one cluster. Nothing anywhere requires the
items to share a driver.

`GraphSignal` already carries the needed field —
`backend/app/scoring/graph_signals.py:58`:

```python
driver: str = ""   # the driving entity name (the convergence is about)
```

It is computed, stored, and then never used for grouping. **The fix is to group by `driver`, not
to invent new machinery.**

Two consequences follow directly:

- **`_publishers()` (`generator.py:72-80`) inflates the source count.** It unions
  `contributing_sources` across all eight unrelated items. The "9 independent sources" is the union
  of nine publishers backing *eight different claims* — not nine sources backing one claim. The
  number is arithmetically correct and rhetorically false.
- **`_theme()` (`generator.py:118-120`) returns `items[0].title`** — the lead item's headline as the
  cluster label. This produces the ungrammatical LinkedIn hook ("*…Agentic orchestration: Enterprise
  AI organizations have a deployment problem, not a platform problem — and most are calling chatbots
  agents* is quietly converging across 9 independent sources") and the meaningless `Ag…` truncation
  on X. With driver-based grouping the label becomes the entity, which is both correct and short.

### Why this specifically matters for this project

The facts in the draft are individually impeccable, sourced, and FACT-labelled. The defect is
entirely in the interpretation layer stacked on top of them. Publishing a Noise Report that calls out
hype, beside a Weak Signal post that overstates convergence using seven irrelevant citations, would
invert the project's own positioning. Per `CLAUDE.md`:

> **Separate source fact from interpretation** in all outputs. Never present an inferred claim as if
> the source stated it. No unsupported claims.

This is that invariant failing in the one place where it is visible to the public.

### Tension with D-014 (needs a ruling)

D-014 records the standing ruling:

> if the quadrant is still empty after the `github_*` ingest, the digest ships with an honest empty
> weak-signal section plus the Noise Report; **no threshold is relaxed to manufacture a signal**.

`horizon_signal` is still **0** (`archive` 208 / `operational_update` 37 / `immediate_priority` 1).

## Finding 2 — The X thread is unpostable (NEW, not previously logged)

Only the **hook** passes through `_fit_hook(...)` and its `X_CHAR_LIMIT`. The thread body is
assembled without any length check (`generator.py:166-169`). In the generated draft, items `2/`
through `9/` are 400–600 character FACT blocks. Nine tweets, none valid.

The draft even self-reports the asymmetry: `**Hook post (≤280 chars — 280):**`. The hook was
measured; nothing else was.

## Finding 3 — The Noise Report is a data dump (NEW, not previously logged)

`outputs/posts/2026-07-21-noise-report-medium.md` is **141 KB across 392 lines** and enumerates all
187 demoted items with no editorial cap. That is a database export with a header, not a Medium post.
LinkedIn and X variants are 16 KB each — same cause.

Conceptually this is the **stronger** of the two pieces. "Named honestly, loudest first, because a
hype-driven feed structurally cannot" is a real, differentiated angle, and every item in it is
individually defensible. It needs a top-N cap (~10–15) and nothing else structural.

## Finding 4 — A non-AI item reached a published artifact

The Noise Report's second entry is an MIT Technology Review opinion piece about **perimenopause**,
scored `hype 5/5`. It scored high because the article is *about* hype — the classifier keyed on
surface hype language, not AI substance.

Two separable questions, previously logged as one:

1. **Source registry.** Is MIT Technology Review subscribed whole-publication rather than
   AI-section-only? A registry fix, cheap.
2. **Classifier.** `prompts/classify_item.md` has no "is this about AI at all" gate. An off-topic
   item cannot be rejected — only scored. Every axis assumes AI-topicality as a given. This is why an
   off-topic item can land at the *top* of a report (high hype = loudest).

(2) is the more interesting finding and is **out of scope** for the task below — it touches the
enrichment contract and deserves its own task file.
